## Indicative course outline

### Introduction & foundations

Students are introduced to the main legal, political and institutional frameworks for ocean governance, moving from global principles to international, regional and national implementation.

- **International ocean governance**  
  Topics include the Law of the Sea, UNCLOS, Areas Beyond National Jurisdiction, the BBNJ Agreement, the Convention on Biological Diversity, global fisheries governance and links between ocean governance and climate policy.

## Student presentations

Individual presentations give students practice in analysing live governance debates and communicating policy arguments to a specialist audience.

- **How well does it work?**  
  Students present a particular institution or framework and assess whether it is fit for purpose and effective, where governance gaps remain, and what reforms may be needed.

- **Is it a good idea?**  
  Students assess a current proposal for a new ocean governance framework or technology, weighing its potential benefits, risks and political feasibility.

- **PechaKucha**  
  Short, visual presentations in the PechaKucha format (20 slides × 20 seconds), giving students a fast and engaging way to communicate complex ocean policy issues clearly and concisely.

## Group exercises

Applied teamwork complements lectures and readings, giving students hands-on experience of the politics and practice of ocean governance.

- **International negotiation simulation**  
  Students take on stakeholder roles in a simulated negotiation on issues such as high seas marine protected areas, seabed mining or biodiversity governance.

- **Marine spatial planning exercise**  
  Students consider how competing uses of marine space can be balanced, including conservation, fisheries, offshore energy, shipping and coastal community interests.

- **Building an ocean rights observatory**  
  Students develop a proposal for an observatory tracking legal and policy developments related to ocean rights, rights of nature and marine governance innovation.

- **Interviewing an expert**  
  Students prepare and conduct an interview with a practitioner or expert, using it to connect course themes to current policy debates and professional practice.
