Basketball is usually told in extremes — genius or fraud, clutch or collapse — or as a highlight reel. Most of what makes the sport meaningful lives elsewhere: routines, awkward footwork, local gyms, long seasons, the quiet ways people carry the game.

Hoops is a field guide to noticing that, not a history, a tactics manual, or a ranking of the usual heroes. It asks what the game *does*: how it shapes attention, creates language, builds temporary communities.

It moves between professional arenas and amateur courts, watching and playing, analysis and memory. There is technique, and also digression.

Like any good field guide, it is incomplete by design — meant to be carried, argued with, set down, picked up again.
