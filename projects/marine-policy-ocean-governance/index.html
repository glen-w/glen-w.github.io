Marine Policy & Ocean Governance

A graduate seminar exploring the political, legal and institutional frameworks governing the ocean. Students examine global, regional and national approaches to ocean governance, with case studies on biodiversity, fisheries, deep sea mining, marine spatial planning, ocean-climate linkages and emerging debates such as rights of nature and the blue economy.

Indicative course outline

Introduction & foundations

Students are introduced to the main legal, political and institutional frameworks for ocean governance, moving from global principles to international, regional and national implementation.

  • International ocean governance
    Topics include the Law of the Sea, UNCLOS, Areas Beyond National Jurisdiction, the BBNJ Agreement, the Convention on Biological Diversity, global fisheries governance and links between ocean governance and climate policy.

    • United Nations (1982). United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea. Montego Bay, 10 December 1982.
    • Koh, Tommy T. B. (1982). A Constitution for the Oceans. Address, Third United Nations Conference on the Law of the Sea, Montego Bay, 10 December 1982.
    • Tladi, Dire (2011). Ocean governance: a fragmented regulatory framework. In P. Jacquet, R. Pachauri and L. Tubiana (eds), Oceans: the new frontier – A planet for life, pp. 99–111. Delhi: Teri Press.

  • Regional ocean governance
    Students examine the role of regional seas organisations, regional fisheries management organisations, regional political bodies and transboundary cooperation in addressing shared marine challenges.

    • Rochette, Julien and Lucien Chabason (2011). A regional approach to marine environmental protection: the “regional seas” experience. In P. Jacquet, R. Pachauri and L. Tubiana (eds), Oceans: the new frontier – A planet for life, pp. 111–121. Delhi: Teri Press.
    • Rochette, Julien et al. (2015). Regional oceans governance mechanisms: A review. Marine Policy 60: 9–19. doi:10.1016/j.marpol.2015.05.012
    • Ásmundsson, Stefán (2016). Regional Fisheries Management Organisations (RFMOs): Who are they, what is their geographic coverage on the high seas and which ones should be considered as General RFMOs, Tuna RFMOs and Specialised RFMOs?
    • Wright, Glen et al. (2017). Partnering for a Sustainable Ocean: The Role of Regional Ocean Governance in Implementing Sustainable Development Goal 14 (Summary for Decision Makers). Partnership for Regional Ocean Governance.
    • Mead, Leila (2021). The “Crown Jewels” of Environmental Diplomacy: Assessing the UNEP Regional Seas Programme. Brief #17, Still Only One Earth: Lessons from 50 years of UN sustainable development policy. International Institute for Sustainable Development.

  • Marine Areas Beyond National Jurisdiction
    Students examine governance of the global ocean commons beyond national jurisdiction, including the negotiation and implementation of the BBNJ Agreement, area-based management tools such as high seas marine protected areas, and the interplay between UNCLOS and emerging conservation frameworks.

    • Wright, Glen et al. (2018). The long and winding road: negotiating a treaty for the conservation and sustainable use of marine biodiversity in areas beyond national jurisdiction. IDDRI Studies N°08/18. Paris: IDDRI.
    • Wright, Glen et al. (2019). High Hopes for the High Seas: beyond the package deal towards an ambitious treaty. IDDRI Issue Brief N°09. Paris: IDDRI.
    • UNESCO World Heritage Centre (2020). Protecting sites of potential Outstanding Universal Value in marine areas beyond national jurisdiction: The practical modalities. Paris: UNESCO.
    • Gjerde, Kristina M. et al. (2022). Getting beyond yes: fast-tracking implementation of the United Nations agreement for marine biodiversity beyond national jurisdiction. npj Ocean Sustainability 1: 6. doi:10.1038/s44183-022-00006-2
    • United Nations (2023). Agreement under the United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea on the conservation and sustainable use of marine biological diversity of areas beyond national jurisdiction. A/CONF.232/2023/4 (19 June 2023).

  • National and local implementation
    The course explores how international commitments are translated into national policy, including marine protected areas, marine spatial planning, fisheries management, coastal governance and stakeholder participation.

Student presentations

Individual presentations give students practice in analysing live governance debates and communicating policy arguments to a specialist audience.

  • How well does it work?
    Students present a particular institution or framework and assess whether it is fit for purpose and effective, where governance gaps remain, and what reforms may be needed.

  • Is it a good idea?
    Students assess a current proposal for a new ocean governance framework or technology, weighing its potential benefits, risks and political feasibility.

  • PechaKucha
    Short, visual presentations in the PechaKucha format (20 slides × 20 seconds), giving students a fast and engaging way to communicate complex ocean policy issues clearly and concisely.

Group exercises

Applied teamwork complements lectures and readings, giving students hands-on experience of the politics and practice of ocean governance.

  • International negotiation simulation
    Students take on stakeholder roles in a simulated negotiation on issues such as high seas marine protected areas, seabed mining or biodiversity governance.

  • Marine spatial planning exercise
    Students consider how competing uses of marine space can be balanced, including conservation, fisheries, offshore energy, shipping and coastal community interests.

  • Building an ocean rights observatory
    Students develop a proposal for an observatory tracking legal and policy developments related to ocean rights, rights of nature and marine governance innovation.

  • Interviewing an expert
    Students prepare and conduct an interview with a practitioner or expert, using it to connect course themes to current policy debates and professional practice.
