It's been a while! A quick note from the editor
This is the first newsletter in 2021! After the fatigue of 2020, I have unfortunately not been able to muster much energy for side projects. It seems that others are feeling the same, as nobody has signed up to curate the newsletter so far this year. Not to worry, I'll keep the project on the backburner, but as the primary purpose of the Letter is to provide a platform for young ocean leaders, there won't be so many editions in the coming months. If you want to write an edition, please feel free to sign up here.
Ocean update 
  • The new head of the World Trade Organization is pushing for members to wrap up the decades-long negotiations on the elimination of harmful fisheries subsidies.
  • The Sargasso Sea Commission has netted $3 million for projects aiming to protect the floating golden rainforest of the Atlantic.
This group of newly hatched loggerhead turtles takes refuge in the sargassum - the golden seaweed unique to the Sargasso Sea.

  • The Economist World Ocean Summit just wrapped up. You can view all the sessions online. See also their recent articles on how investors can finance a sustainable ocean economy and nature-based solutions can save coral reefs.
  • New UNESCO research shows that the 50 marine sites on UNESCO World Heritage List cover at least 21% of the global area of blue carbon ecosystems - carbon assets equivalent to about 10% of global greenhouse gas emissions.
