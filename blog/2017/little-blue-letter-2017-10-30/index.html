30 October 2017 — Food for thought

Created on October 30, 2017

2017   ·   LittleBlueLetter   ·   Little Blue Letter

Sent 30 October 2017. Part of Little Blue Letter.

"The health of our oceans is under threat.
They’re changing at a faster rate than ever before in human history."

Before the boring bits, let me say this: Blue Planet II is truly awesome. The first episode, was a masterclass in science and cinematography. If you are in the UK, make BPII your Sunday evening ritual; if you aren't in the UK, you can still see the stunning trailer, check out some clips, and listen to the accompanying podcast. The BBC has also setup a website giving some conservation pointers to those inspired by the show. 

The Convention on Migratory Species wrapped up last week in Manila - the largest meeting in its 38-year history. A record number of commitments were made; three species of shark and three species of ray will receive greater protection. The Philippines was commended for its efforts to protect whale sharks, which are essential to livelihoods in the region.

Things moved more slowly in London, where the International Maritime Organisation (IMO), "made progress in starting to shape a draft initial IMO greenhouse gas strategy". The aim is to reach consensus at the next meeting and to agree short-term measures between 2018-2023. Hardly groundbreaking, but it's a start.

A couple of weeks back, a shocking photo of plastic pollution in Honduras made the rounds online. Following a flurry of social media attention, the President of Honduras has appointed a task force and Guatemala, from where most of the litter comes, committed $2 million to building a 'waste-trap' to stem the flow. In the meantime, Honduras is asking for compensation. Perhaps we need an international agreement on marine plastic pollution?

#OceanOptimism
The Labor government of Queensland, Australia has banned the loading of coal ships at sea in the Great Barrier Reef marine park. Capturing just 2% of the planet's wind energy would meet all our energy needs. Hi-tech tools could prevent whale collisions. Tuna are not radioactive.
Food for thought
What if dolphins got Alzheimer's? What if we treated whales as stakeholders?
