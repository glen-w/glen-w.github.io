Ice ice baby
Commercial sea-ways are the backbone of global trade, but just one giant container ship emits as much pollutants as 50 million cars! That’s why the 2017 represents a milestone in the protection of the Arctic Seas and their marine environment.

This year, the International Maritime Organization (IMO) (finally!) adopted the International Code for Ships Operating in Polar Waters (the 'Polar Code' for short). The agreement, much of which came into force in January, establishes mandatory standards for ships operating in the icy waters of the Arctic and Antarctic.
The Polar Code is worth celebrating - it is the first code specifically designed for the regulation of navigation in the Northern Seas and, above all, it sets specific standards to be respected by ships for the protection of the fragile Arctic ecosystem. In other words, the Polar Code could be the first legal instrument that supports safe and environmentally-friendly shipping in the Arctic and Antarctic waters, which will be among the most affected by climate change.
 
There is still a long way to go to fully protect our precious polar regions... but well begun is half done! For more info, take a look at this video.
Gif of the week
Known for its distinctive dark spots and saddle type markings, the Leopard Shark is known to live up to 30 years and takes more than a decade to reach maturity. It is small, generally harmless to humans, and is easily spooked, so many divers and snorkelers have a tough time catching a glimpse before they swim off.

From the editor
Last week I jokingly referred to ICCAT as the International Conspiracy to Catch All Tuna. This week, ICCAT members approved a considerable increase in the catch quota for bluefin tuna. EU Commissioner Karmenu Vella said that the decision was in line with scientific advice, while others have called it disgraceful and unacceptable as it puts long-term recovery and sustainability at risk.
