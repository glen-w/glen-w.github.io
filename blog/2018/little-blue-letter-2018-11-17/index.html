Wetlands in the world spotlight
The Ramsar Convention on Wetlands - an intergovernmental treaty that provides a framework for national action and international cooperation for the conservation and wise use of wetlands and their resources - recently met in Dubai. The Convention was established over 40 years ago to protect wetlands around the world and there are now more than 2,000 Ramsar-designated wetlands that represent rare or unique habitats important for conserving biological diversity.
The Convention just issued the first-ever global report on the state of the world's wetlands, finding that about 35% of the planet’s wetlands were lost between 1970 and 2015. This is concerning as wetlands mitigate floods, protect coastlines and build community resilience to disasters, and play an important role in transport, tourism and culture. They are also a vital source of food (60%-90% of U.S. commercial fisheries depend on wetlands) and provide the largest terrestrial carbon store.

Around the world people are helping us understand the value of wetlands in order to develop better ways of protecting and restoring these important habitats. Recently, a team of interdisciplinary researchers (including the insurance industry!) calculated the financial benefits that coastal wetlands provide by reducing damages from hurricanes and offers new evidence that protecting natural ecosystems is a cost-effective way of reducing risks from flooding. Another team of researchers suggests the idea of “Wetland Restoration Brokers” - independent mediators that could help settle conflicts among stakeholders or between stakeholders and the environment. And the Ramsar Convention itself has launched accreditation for cities that conserve wetlands, with 18 cities around the world so far recognized.
