Taking cues from nature
Birds naturally gather around fishing vessels, so researchers are exploring the possibility of turning them into spies against illegal, unreported, and unregulated fishing (IUU). Albatross are fitted with tracking devices that can record the location nearby boats in international waters.

Scientists have identified a marine bacteria that loves to eat hydrocarbons, which could be the answer to oil spill clean-ups. Its enzymes clean soil and water contaminated by petroleum-based products in a simple, effective, and environmentally-friendly manner.

Mantis shrimp have inspired a new mode of underwater navigation using the polarization of light that allows more accurate and cost-effective long-distance navigation. Humans can only perceive polarized light with special lenses like those in sunglasses.

#OceanOptimism
  • After massive volunteer cleanup efforts, Olive Ridley turtle hatchlings have been spotted for the first time in decade on a Mumbai beach.
  • A supermarket bag levy has been linked to decline in plastic bags around the British seabeds. Scientists estimate there has been a 30% drop in plastic bag pollution.
