No waste, no problem
Vietnam is facing an influx of imported plastic trash as China's recently enacted “Green Fence” policy has caused an exodus of recycling companies from the country. Yet many Vietnamese cities are already facing huge waste management crises: landfills are filling much faster than expected, leaving city governments scrambling to come up with a plan B. One quick fix has been incineration, but many substandard incinerators release toxic chemicals into the air.
An employee in the central province of Quang Binh tries to control a fire that raged for 10 days in a landfill, causing serious smoke pollution in the area and affecting hundreds of nearby households.
(VNA/VNS Photo Duc Tho)
 
Local environmental trailblazers are on the task, with international collaborators helping out. In June, the marine program at Pacific Environment joined local organizations in Ha Long City to conduct a household waste audit. Together, they collected, sorted, quantified, and recorded brand contributors daily from 60 households. Nearly 2/3 of household waste was organic, so it could be composted; 1/4 was recyclable; and only 12% truly needed to go to landfill. There are therefore many potential solutions, such as product bans or alternatives to single-use packaging that could further reduce the stream of waste headed to landfills.
Many grassroots organizations in Vietnam are advocating for zero waste. Cheap, practical, and sustainable, this solution targets the root of the issue: the over-consumption of disposable materials.
