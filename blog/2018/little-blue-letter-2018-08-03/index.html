3 August 2018 — The plight of the penguins

Created on August 03, 2018

2018   ·   LittleBlueLetter   ·   Little Blue Letter

Sent 3 August 2018. Part of Little Blue Letter.

"I have always felt I lived on the high seas, threatened, at the heart of a royal happiness."
~ Albert Camus

The ocean wilds
Just 13% of the world's oceans remain as wilderness, researchers have found - a stark reminder of the pressures our global oceans are facing from climate change, industrial fishing and pollution. Most of these ocean wildernesses are on the high seas, the international waters that belong to us all and cover an area bigger than every continent combined.
Governments will meet this September to start negotiating a new international that - if ambitious enough - could help set up a network of marine protected areas on the high seas. Check out the High Seas Alliance for all you need to know on this major opportunity for ocean protection.

The plight of the penguins
Governments will also decide later this year whether to protect 1.8 million square kilometres of the Weddell Sea, making it off-limits to industry. Almost 2 million people around the world are now calling for an Antarctic Ocean Sanctuary - including a penguin that got lost in China.
Mining the deep
It's been a big couple of weeks for deep sea mining discussions, but navigating the depths of the acronyms and the big unknowns is a mammoth task. Here’s a handy guide of the essentials and an update on all the unanswered questions to get you up to date. Last month, 45 environmental organisations warned that deep sea mining could could wipe out species and ecosystems before we know they exist. Even the Pope is getting involved, urging governments to prioritise ocean health and human life.
Quick takes
  • We shouldn't have called it ‘Earth’.

  • Big data is helping to detect hidden crimes on the high seas, with satellite research into transhipment shining a light on the potential for illegal fishing and human rights abuses in some of the most remote waters.
