HFO, GHG, IMO, OMG
If shipping were a country, it’d be the sixth largest emitter of CO2 (right between Japan and Germany). So it’s exciting news that last April, the International Maritime Organization (IMO), the United Nations body responsible for developing environmental and safety regulations for international shipping, took steps to reduce the industry’s footprint.
Activists powered washed “IMO DONT SINK PARIS” onto a retaining wall directly across the River Thames from the IMO. (Credit: Campaign Against Climate Change)
 
Most notably, the IMO committed to reducing future greenhouse gas emissions by at least 50% by 2050 compared to 2008 levels, which aligns with the goals of the Paris Agreement. It’s not as high as some countries and environmental organizations were seeking, but the “at least” language leaves room for much improvement.

Another win for the ocean and the climate came with the commitment to develop a ban on heavy fuel oil (HFO) use in the Arctic, which accounts for 75% of fuel used in the region. HFO is a thick, tar-like sludge that emits black carbon, which can coat ice and speed up melting. In the event of a spill in Arctic waters, HFO would be nearly impossible to clean up.

#OceanOptimism
It can be hard to find optimistic news when talking about the polar regions of the world in the context of climate change, but this past week, we got some: humpback whales in the Antarctic are making a comeback! Once hunted to near extinction, researchers are seeing increasing rates of pregnant females.
