Outlaw ocean
A new book, The Outlaw Ocean, has been making waves in the past couple of weeks. Written by award-winning New York Times investigative journalist Ian Urbina, it exposes the "last frontier", a "strange world that few on land ever experience" (Vice article; Guardian long read). World Ocean Radio, is running a four-part series, including readings from the book. It's not just illegal fishing and slave labour either: Mexican drug baron ‘El Chapo’ used a US seafood firm to launder drug money and Chinese customs officials once found cocaine being smuggled in shipments of frozen mackerel!

High hopes for the high seas
The third round of negotiations for a new treaty on high seas biodiversity just wrapped up in New York. The Earth Negotiations Bulletin notes, "delegates patted themselves on the back for having gone through the major elements of the draft text... but acknowledged that more needs to be done to address the pressing matters..." Yet many from the conservation community see the negotiations as moving far too slowly and with little ambition for the strong treaty needed to protect our planet's last wilderness. What would an ambitious treaty look like? This IDDRI policy brief has some suggestions.

IMarEST Ballast Water Special Interest Group 
Invasive species are one of the biggest threats to global biodiversity and the transfer of these non-native species through ships’ ballast water – used to improve stability at sea – is a huge culprit for their transportation to new waters around the world. With the continued growth of the international shipping sector, there is a global need to address the issue.  With consultative status at the International Maritime Organisation (IMO), the IMarEST Ballast Water Management expert group/SIG works towards reducing the industry’s spread of alien species by contributing to the development of the Ballast Water Management Convention which is now in its experience building phase and implementation due in 2020.

​IMarEST is an international learned society and membership body that brings marine engineers, scientists and technologists together. Its Special Interest Groups (SIGs) provide a platform for knowledge-sharing, outreach and advisory information for the wider industry – in some cases influencing legislation and policy. You can find out more information on the Special Interest Group and its outputs, alongside other IMarEST groups and how to get involved on the IMarEST website.
