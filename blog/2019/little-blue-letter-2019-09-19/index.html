"Everyday is World Oceans Day for me. I'm either in, on or around ocean or I'm thinking about how to drive change. I feel intensely responsible about leaving this world a better place I found it."
~ Dr. Asha de Vos
Seaweed invasion
Pristine beaches, clear waters, palm trees, sea turtles... and a massive brown carpet of foul-smelling algae. An invasion of dead Sargassum seaweed is destroying marine life and causing significant losses to fishermen and the tourist industry in Caribbean islands and the Gulf of Mexico. 
The stringy brown seaweed is usually found floating in large mats in the Sargasso Sea, where it provides vital breeding and feeding grounds for marine life. It is not yet clear what has caused the disproportionately huge bloom, but global warming and agricultural runoffs are considered important factors.

When washed ashore at beaches, seaweed mats as deep as seven meters start to decompose, causing damage to sea turtle populations whose nesting sites become blocked or damaged by removal work. In the recent UNCTAD Raul Prebisch Lecture (presented by a woman for the first time!), the Prime Minister of Barbados, Mia Mottley, warned the global community of the growing problem, highlighting a lack of resources for small islands to cope with this challenge. 

Fortunately, people are finding ingenious solutions. Barbados has repurposed old sugar cane trucks to pick sargassum without removing sand from the beaches. A company in Guadeloupe has developed a boat equipped with a conveyor belt to collect the weeds quickly. From Plant tonics to fertilizers to goat feed, other commercial uses are being explored.

#OceanOptimis
  • These five incredible female ocean activists walk the talk, including Sri Lankan researcher Dr. Asha de Vos, who convinces you in 5 minutes that whale poo is cool! 
  • Coral gardeners bring back Jamaica’s reefs by painstakingly tending to their coral nurseries. Some of them are fishermen who came to understand the vital linkage between flourishing coral reefs and healthy fish stocks. 
  • The tiny Central American country of Belize is a world leader in protecting the ocean. In close collaboration with the fishing community, a managed access fishing programme restricts ‘open access’ fishing through the distribution of licenses, thereby reducing pressure on vital fish stocks and the incentive to fish illegally.  
  • Salmon farming in the Patagonian Sea is facing a growing wave of resistance due to its environmentally damaging and polluting practices. Campaigners have now put a stop to one company’s plans to rear 1.9m fish in the Beagle Channel between Chile and Argentina.
