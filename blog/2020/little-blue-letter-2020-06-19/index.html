Will the pandemic fast-track seabed mining?
  • The ongoing coronavirus pandemic has disrupted negotiations for developing seabed mining regulations
  • One company, Deep Green, says it is considering invoking a rule that would compel approval of mining plans within 2 years.
  • The head of the International Seabed Authority (ISA), the body responsible for passing a mining code, has been surprisingly vocal in his opposition to a moratorium on seabed mining, saying that it would be 'anti-science, anti-knowledge, anti-development, and anti-international law'.
  • "It is crucial to ensure that the current crisis is not used as an excuse to ‘fast-track’ any decision-making by curtailing the prevailing norms of due process, inclusivity and good governance”, says Duncan Currie, international lawyer and member of the Deep Sea Conservation Coalition
  • The Secretary-General of the ISA is a self-described "advocate for rule of law in the ocean", but his independence has been called into question.
