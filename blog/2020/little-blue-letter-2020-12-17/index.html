Drift gillnets to be phased out in California
The swordfish and thresher shark fishery in California will be phasing out the use of drift gillnets over the course of the next four years. Drift gillnets can be more than a mile long and reach up to 60 m (200 ft) depth from the sea surface. They are often left overnight, resulting in entanglement of various non-target species.

Around 60% of the species caught by the driftnet fishery are listed as threatened or endangered by the IUCN. The law that was passed this week aims to replace the drift gillnets with deep-set buoy gear, which will be baited for swordfish and signal to fishermen when a bite is detected. Trials of the deep-set buoy gear have shown catch consisting of over 80% swordfish compared with traditional drift gillnet gear. Under the new law, fishermen will receive a $100,000 one-off payment to surrender their nets to be destroyed, and if they choose to surrender their permit a further $10,000.

OceanOptimism
New species of beaked whale may have been discovered off the coast of Mexico. Three beaked whales were spotted north of San Benito Islands. Upon closer inspection of photo ID scientists realised that one of the animals did not present characteristics matching any of the described species of beaked whales. Water samples were taken to use eDNA for confirmation of the species identity.

Making Black (Women’s) Lives Matter in Marine science 
Dr Amelia Moore’s poignant and illuminating essay on the conspicuous absence of women of colour from the field of Marine Science is recommended reading for any young scientist. #BlackinMarineScienceWeek and  other initiatives have been improving the visibility of BIPOC in the field. However, we have to go much farther in order to increase accessibility. The onus and responsibility for that should not fall on the shoulders on the marginalised, it’s up to us.
