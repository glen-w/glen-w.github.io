2020: a bumper year for the ocean?
The “Blue COP” in December 2019 marked the end of a busy year in ocean policy, which is now high on the international agenda (other highlights for me include the IPCC Special Report and the first Marine Regions Forum). The ocean community is gearing up for another big year in 2020, with several important events and political deadlines on the horizon:
  • The second UN Ocean Conference (Lisbon, June) will pick up where the first edition left off in 2017, with the goal of assessing the implementation of SDG14 and spurring action. But will it result in ambitious commitments or simply add yet another political declaration and unkept promises?
  • The 15th Conference of the Parties of the Convention on Biological Diversity (Kunming, October) will seek to adopt a Post-2020 Global Biodiversity Framework (update on progress here). It could include ambitious ocean targets, including protected area coverage (30%? by 2030?).
  • The long process to negotiate a treaty to protect the high seas—which covers nearly half of the Earth’s surface and does not belong to any one nation—could conclude this year. All eyes are now on the final round of negotiations at the end of March (UN Headquarters, New York).
  • The World Trade Organization could finally agree to remove harmful fisheries subsidies.
  • We will also be closely following the IUCN World Conservation Congress, the process to develop seabed mining regulations, the Our Ocean conference, the European Green Deal, and climate COP26.

Ocean opportunities
  • The Partnership for Observation of the Global Ocean (POGO) is issuing an open call for applications from any early career scientists involved in oceanographic work at centres in developing countries and countries with economies in transition.
  • The Conservation Finance Alliance Incubator is seeking applications (5 grants of $25,000 available).
  • The Ocean Innovation Challenge is looking for innovative solutions to counter ocean pollution.
