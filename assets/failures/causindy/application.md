1. How have you been involved in the Australia-Indonesia relationship so far, and what perspectives do you think you can bring to the Conference? My involvement in the Australia-Indonesia relationship so far has been through learning the Indonesian language and conversing with young Indonesians. During a 2 week period over Christmas 2012 I attended an intensive language course in Bali, receiving four hours of one-on-one tuition each day. I undertook this course of study voluntarily during my holidays in order to further my personal interest in Indonesia. I have since continued weekly lessons and regularly arrange language exchange sessions to speak with young Indonesians. During these sessions, I have the opportunity to practice my Indonesian and assist them in learning English, while also learning more about Indonesia. I also read the news in Indonesian to stay abreast of current affairs and improve my language skills. I am keen to extend my involvement and to learn more about our closest neighbour. I have recently joined the ANU’s Indonesia Study Group and hope to attend the lunchtime study sessions. Strengthening relations in a changing geopolitical contextI believe the biggest issue in the bilateral relationship will be the improvement and maintenance of the relationship itself as a strategic response to changing dynamics both globally and within the region. Indonesia and Australia are natural strategic partners but at times the relationship has been antagonistic, whereas more effort is required to strengthen the relationship over the next 10-15 years. In a changing world with the US and China as the major powers, Australia and Indonesia may not wish to be closely allied to either, and would benefit from being closely allied to each other in order to mutually advance their respective interests and provide a balance to this emerging power.
