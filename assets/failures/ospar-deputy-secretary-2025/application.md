Briefing Note
OSPAR and Bonn: Strengthening Pollution Prevention in the North-East Atlantic

Pollution from offshore activities, radioactive substances and shipping crosses borders, involves shared sea basins and often emerges from patterns that no individual state can detect alone. National permitting systems, EU directives and IMO/IAEA rules each address part of the problem, but they operate at different scales, with differing data, enforcement cultures and legal frameworks.
The result is a set of strong tools that nevertheless leave gaps where jurisdictions meet. OSPAR and the Bonn Agreement exist to close those gaps. We provide the regional architecture that links scientific evidence to standards, standards to enforcement, and enforcement to practical response. We provide an essential layer of cooperation that ensures national efforts result in a coherent and effective system of prevention and preparedness for the North-East Atlantic.

A Regional System of Shared Evidence and Standards
The foundation of effective pollution prevention is a shared picture of the sea basin. OSPAR’s coordinated monitoring and assessment programmes give all states the same scientific baselines, built on harmonised sampling, common indicators and jointly agreed methods. That evidence reveals trends that simply cannot be seen from a national vantage point, whether in radiological materials, offshore chemicals or microlitter.
OSPAR establishes region-wide standards that provide clarity and consistency in the application of global rules. The regional ban on scrubber discharges is a clear example: a regulatory grey area that created uneven implementation at ports and along shipping routes is now subject to a coherent regional approach that strengthens national enforcement and sets a model for future EU or IMO consideration. Likewise, the application of Best Available Techniques to radioactive discharges ensures rigorous environmental standards, as well as transparency and verification that builds trust between nuclear and non-nuclear states.

Turning Rules into Practical Enforcement and Operational Capability
Strong standards do not deliver results without strong enforcement. The Bonn
Agreement translates global obligations (particularly MARPOL Annex I & VI) into workable operational systems. Coordinated aerial and satellite surveillance, shared intelligence in BADIMS and the collective analysis undertaken with the North Sea Network of Prosecutors all enhance the detection and prosecution of illegal discharges. No single state has the coverage or intelligence needed to achieve this effect alone; the regional layer provides both scale and continuity.
Crucially, the Bonn Agreement and OSPAR also ensure states can respond when incidents occur. Rapid alerts, shared assets, common manuals and regular joint exercises give countries the ability to act quickly and coherently across borders. These arrangements are not theoretical: they are used, tested and improved continuously. Global frameworks provide the obligations; the regional system provides the capability.

### A Regional System for Risk Management

When taken together, OSPAR and the Bonn Agreement create a full-cycle risk management system: Prevention is strengthened through harmonised standards and shared scientific baselines; Preparedness is shaped by coordinated surveillance schedules, joint risk assessments and interoperable procedures; Response is delivered through rapid mobilisation and consistent on-scene operations; Recovery and learning are embedded through OSPAR assessments, Bonn Agreement debriefs and prosecutorial reviews that feed directly into updated standards and guidance.
This cycle produces something no individual authority can achieve: a shared regional learning system where improvements in one area strengthen the whole.

Complementing National, EU and Global Efforts
The value added by regional cooperation is not substitution but coherence. For national regulators, regional evidence and aligned standards reduce administrative burden and improve the consistency of permitting, oversight and enforcement.
For the EU, OSPAR provides stable, region-wide inputs that support implementation of the Marine Strategy Framework Directive and the Offshore Safety Directive.
For global bodies, regional arrangements act as the bridge between high-level rules and operational reality. They demonstrate what effective implementation looks like, often informing future policy development at the IMO and IAEA.
As monitoring, surveillance and reporting become more digitalised, the ability of regional organisations to integrate and interpret information across borders will become even more important.

### Regional Cooperation to Tackle Emerging Issues

Several developments on the horizon will stretch national systems and reinforce the need for strong regional cooperation.
•   Decommissioning of offshore infrastructure and nuclear facilities requires consistent environmental guidance and shared baselines to manage cumulative impacts.
•   The ageing tanker fleet and rise of dark shipping demand more coordinated surveillance and stronger intelligence-sharing networks.
•   Climate change is altering storm patterns and spill dynamics, requiring updated scenarios and exercises.
•   Pollutants such as PFAS, microplastics and underwater noise need harmonised approaches so states can detect trends early and develop effective mitigation.
These challenges all share the same features: cross-border effects, shared operational risks and significant uncertainty. They are precisely the issues for which regional cooperation delivers the greatest added value.
