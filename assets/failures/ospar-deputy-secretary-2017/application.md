Application for Deputy Secretary, Biodiversity

To the Executive Secretariat of the OSPAR Commission,
I am writing to apply for the post of Deputy Secretary, Biodiversity, at the OSPAR Commission. I understand that biodiversity is a varied and growing portfolio for OSPAR, and I am keen to assist parties in meeting their treaty obligations and advancing their environmental objectives.
I am driven and ambitious, with extensive knowledge of marine policy issues, particularly the regional approach to ocean governance. I am currently a Research Fellow in International Marine Policy at the Institut du Développement Durable et des Relations Internationales (IDDRI, Sciences Po) in Paris, where I lead our activities on areas beyond national jurisdiction (ABNJ) and a project on regional governance in the Southwest Indian Ocean.
I have also played an instrumental role in the conception and formation of the Partnership for Regional Ocean Governance (PROG), a collaboration with the German Government, UNEP, and other partners. We are now finalising funding for this ambitious project and developing a suite of activities on regional ocean governance, ABNJ, and the implementation of SDG 14.
I am excited by the prospect of working within a leading Regional Seas programme to protect and conserve the North-East Atlantic, and I believe that my passion and experience will make a significant contribution to the Commission’s work on biodiversity.

### Knowledge of marine policy

I have studied many international environmental law subjects, and during the course of my doctoral studies I have gained an in-depth knowledge and understanding of a range of oceans governance issues, including: EU regulation (e.g. the Marine Strategy Framework Directive, the Common Fisheries Policy, and the MSP and EIA Directives), MSP, marine licensing, ICZM, EIA, and offshore oil and gas regulation. I am regularly invited to lecture on marine governance and policy at courses run by the International Ocean Institute and at Sciences Po, and am a member of the IUCN World Commission on Environmental Law. In writing an initial report for our SDG project, I have gained extensive knowledge of the efforts of OSPAR and other regional governance frameworks. I have learned about the OSPAR MPA network and the “collective arrangement”, and have read key publications on these topics, including the 2012 assessment of ecological coherence, the 2014 status report, and the recently published information paper on the collective arrangement. In the context of our Southwest Indian Ocean project, I have extensively researched existing efforts to provide for cross-sectoral management in ABNJ and have written a report on potential options for the region.
My involvement in the ongoing ABNJ discussions has strengthened my knowledge of the UN system and international processes. I believe the ABNJ negotiations will be of increasing relevance and interest to Regional Seas programmes, and I would look forward to using my knowledge and experience to support OSPAR in this process.
I am deeply interested in marine science and have made considerable efforts to further my understanding of this area. I am enrolled in the University of Exeter’s distance learning course on marine biology, due to commence in October. I will also attend the 2016 CommOcean conference and training day with the aim of ensuring that my policy work is linked to the relevant science and that I am able to communicate research outcomes in an efficient and effective manner.
At IDDRI, collaboration across different teams is strongly encouraged, and at OSPAR I would be keen to work with other Deputy Secretaries, for example on offshore industry and human activities, to find synergies and ensure the completeness of the biodiversity portfolio.

Vacancy Announcement and Job Description for the post of Deputy Secretary, Biodiversity

1.    Applications are invited from suitably qualified candidates for the post of Deputy Secretary in the Secretariat of the OSPAR Commission.
2.    Under the Commission’s Staff Rules, the appointment will be for an initial period of three years (subject to an initial period of probation). After a review at the end of that period, a second three‐ year contract may then be offered. There is a maximum period of service of six years, except in special circumstances.
3.   The successful candidate will be asked to take up his /her post sometime from beginning of August 2017. The post is based in the Secretariat offices in central London.
4.    The post is open to nationals of the Contracting Parties to the OSPAR Convention, that is, nationals from the following countries: Belgium, Denmark, Finland, France, Germany, Iceland, Ireland, Luxembourg, the Netherlands, Norway, Portugal, Spain, Sweden, Switzerland and the United Kingdom and nationals of any other State which is a Member State of the European Union. The OSPAR Commission
5.    The OSPAR Commission is an international intergovernmental organisation established to protect the marine environment in the North‐East Atlantic Ocean. It consists of representatives of the Contracting Parties: Belgium, Denmark, Finland, France, Germany, Iceland, Ireland, Luxembourg, the Netherlands, Norway, Portugal, Spain, Sweden, Switzerland, the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland and the European Union. The Commission is administered by a Secretariat based in London, which also supports a sister organisation, the Bonn Agreement.
6.   The Secretariat consists of an Executive Secretary, four Deputy Secretaries and six administrative assistants. The working languages are English and French.
7.     The responsibilities of the Secretariat are to help the Commission develop and implement its policies and regulation. This includes the preparation of, and assistance at, the meetings of the Commission and its subsidiary bodies, the preparation of reports and documents for these meetings, general secretariat duties connected with the smooth running of the Commission’s activities and relations with the public, the media and inter‐governmental and non‐governmental organisations.

Description of the post and requirements
9.  Deputy Secretaries are expected to make themselves familiar with the full range of the Commission’s activities and to work to ensure integration between the different themes of work.
10. The work involves advising the relevant committees and working groups of the Commission, ensuring the preparation of the documents necessary for their meetings (including drawing up technical reports, supervising the compilation of statistical reports for publication and editing draft background documents on specific problems), writing the summary records of those meetings and ensuring appropriate follow‐up to the conclusions of the meetings. The person appointed to this particular role is responsible for the many strands of work that are under OSPAR’s Biodiversity Strategy and the current Deputy Secretary has also been leading on OSPAR’s Data and Information Management Strategy.
11. The person to be appointed will have attained a general educational standard equivalent to a good university degree, preferably in environmental management, natural or environmental sciences and will normally have had at least five years' work experience in environmental policy development and implementation.
12.   The principal abilities required include:
      a good working knowledge of environmental policy and its regulatory basis, with particular reference to the marine environment;
      an understanding of the marine environment from an ecosystem perspective;       knowledge of practical, legal and institutional aspects of intergovernmental organisations;
      the ability to address the application of environmental knowledge and information for the purposes of OSPAR’s objectives and on‐going work;
      representational skills to present material to the Commission and its subsidiary bodies and to represent the views and policies of the Commission in other forums;       sound organisational and administrative skills (including IT skills, among which should be familiarity with word‐processing, spread‐sheets and data management)      experience that would help to further develop the Data and Information strategy within OSPAR would be an advantage.
13. It is expected that the person appointed will ideally have particular experience or knowledge of marine environmental monitoring and assessment and/or implementation of policies relating to marine biodiversity and ecosystems. Knowledge of relevant International or European marine regulation and policy making is also relevant.
14. The bulk of the Secretariat's work is conducted in English, but documents, reports and publications are also produced in French and some of the correspondence is written in French. A thorough knowledge of English is therefore essential, as is the ability to draft quickly, accurately and concisely in English. Given equal professional qualifications, preference will be given to candidates who also have a good knowledge of French.
15. Deputy Secretaries are required to travel frequently within Europe and to accept irregular working hours.
