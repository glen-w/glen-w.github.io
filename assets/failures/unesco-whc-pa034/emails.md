What is UNESCO's role in marine conservation?

The primary objective of the 1972 World Heritage Convention is to protect cultural and natural heritage of "outstanding universal value" (OUV).

UNESCO's role in marine conservation is therefore to ensure that:
    1.    Sites in the marine environment that host cultural and natural heritage of OUV are identified and inscribed on the World Heritage List; and
    2.    The marine biodiversity of these sites is protected, along with their associated cultural, spiritual and economic values.

A recent example of UNESCO's role can be seen in the case of the Banc d'Arguin heritage site, where UNESCO is working with the Government of Mauritania to seek designation as a "Particularly Sensitive Sea Area" (PSSA) under the International Maritime Organisation (IMO) guidelines, and the adoption of associated protective measures to mitigate the potential impacts from shipping.

Similarly, the Programme has worked with the Government of Belize to support their efforts to remove the Belize Barrier Reef from the List of World Heritage in Danger, resulting in the prohibition of oil and gas exploration in the site.

In addition to these core roles, UNESCO also plays a complementary role in supporting national governments and authorities in their conservation efforts. Through its World Heritage Marine Programme, established in 2005, UNESCO ensures that World Heritage Marine sites represent exemplary models of conservation and good governance, and provides capacity building and training, in addition to knowledge sharing and networking between site managers. For example, the Programme has established a network of site managers that provides networking opportunities and a space to share best practices and experiences.

The Bahrain Action Plan for Marine World Heritage (2010) envisages that these roles will be strengthened and expanded, for example through improving data on marine protected areas and reviewing Tentative Lists to assist States in the inscription of new sites in gap provinces.

What is UNESCO’s role in marine conservation?

The primary objective of the 1972 World Heritage Convention is to protect cultural and natural heritage of “outstanding universal value” (OUV).

UNESCO’s role in marine conservation is therefore to ensure that:
    1.    Sites in the marine environment that host cultural and natural heritage of OUV are identified and inscribed on the World Heritage List; and
    2.    The marine biodiversity of these sites is protected, along with their associated cultural, spiritual and economic values.

A recent example of UNESCO’s role can be seen in the case of the Banc d’Arguin heritage site, where UNESCO is working with the Government of Mauritania to seek designation as a “Particularly Sensitive Sea Area” (PSSA) under the International Maritime Organisation (IMO) guidelines, and the adoption of associated protective measures to mitigate the potential impacts from shipping.

Similarly, the Programme has worked with the Government of Belize to support their efforts to remove the Belize Barrier Reef from the List of World Heritage in Danger, resulting in the prohibition of oil and gas exploration in the site.

In addition to these core roles, UNESCO also plays a complementary role in supporting national governments and authorities in their conservation efforts. Through its World Heritage Marine Programme, established in 2005, UNESCO ensures that World Heritage Marine sites represent exemplary models of conservation and good governance, and provides capacity building and training, in addition to knowledge sharing and networking between site managers. For example, the Programme has established a network of site managers that provides networking opportunities and a space to share best practices and experiences.

The Bahrain Action Plan for Marine World Heritage (2010) envisages that these roles will be strengthened and expanded, for example through improving data on marine protected areas and reviewing Tentative Lists to assist States in the inscription of new sites in gap provinces.

What is UNESCO's role in marine conservation?

The primary objective of the 1972 World Heritage Convention is to protect cultural and natural heritage of "outstanding universal value" (OUV).

UNESCO's role in marine conservation is therefore to ensure that:

     1.    Sites in the marine environment that host cultural and natural heritage of OUV are identified and inscribed on the World Heritage List; and

     2.    The marine biodiversity of these sites is protected, along with their associated cultural, spiritual and economic values.

A recent example of UNESCO's role can be seen in the case of the Banc d'Arguin heritage site, where UNESCO is working with the Government of Mauritania to seek designation as a "Particularly Sensitive Sea Area" (PSSA) under the International Maritime Organisation (IMO) guidelines, and the adoption of associated protective measures to mitigate the potential impacts from shipping.

Similarly, the Programme has worked with the Government of Belize to support their efforts to remove the Belize Barrier Reef from the List of World Heritage in Danger, resulting in the prohibition of oil and gas exploration in the site.

In addition to these core roles, UNESCO also plays a complementary role in supporting national governments and authorities in their conservation efforts. Through its World Heritage Marine Programme, established in 2005, UNESCO ensures that World Heritage Marine sites represent exemplary models of conservation and good governance, and provides capacity building and training, in addition to knowledge sharing and networking between site managers. For example, the Programme has established a network of site managers that provides networking opportunities and a space to share best practices and experiences.

The Bahrain Action Plan for Marine World Heritage (2010) envisages that these roles will be strengthened and expanded, for example through improving data on marine protected areas and reviewing Tentative Lists to assist States in the inscription of new sites in gap provinces.

What is UNESCO's role in marine conservation?

The primary objective of the 1972 World Heritage Convention is to protect cultural and natural heritage of "outstanding universal value" (OUV).

UNESCO's role in marine conservation is therefore to ensure that:

     1.    Sites in the marine environment that host cultural and natural heritage of OUV are identified and inscribed on the World Heritage List; and

     2.    The marine biodiversity of these sites is protected, along with their associated cultural, spiritual and economic values.

A recent example of UNESCO's role can be seen in the case of the Banc d'Arguin heritage site, where UNESCO is working with the Government of Mauritania to seek designation as a "Particularly Sensitive Sea Area" (PSSA) under the International Maritime Organisation (IMO) guidelines, and the adoption of associated protective measures to mitigate the potential impacts from shipping.

Similarly, the Programme has worked with the Government of Belize to support their efforts to remove the Belize Barrier Reef from the List of World Heritage in Danger, resulting in the prohibition of oil and gas exploration in the site.

In addition to these core roles, UNESCO also plays a complementary role in supporting national governments and authorities in their conservation efforts. Through its World Heritage Marine Programme, established in 2005, UNESCO ensures that World Heritage Marine sites represent exemplary models of conservation and good governance, and provides capacity building and training, in addition to knowledge sharing and networking between site managers. For example, the Programme has established a network of site managers that provides networking opportunities and a space to share best practices and experiences.

The Bahrain Action Plan for Marine World Heritage (2010) envisages that these roles will be strengthened and expanded, for example through improving data on marine protected areas and reviewing Tentative Lists to assist States in the inscription of new sites in gap provinces.

The primary objective of the 1972 World Heritage Convention is to protect cultural and natural heritage of "outstanding universal value" (OUV).

UNESCO's role in marine conservation is therefore to ensure that:

     1.    Sites in the marine environment that host cultural and natural heritage of OUV are identified and inscribed on the World Heritage List; and

A recent example of UNESCO's role can be seen in the case of the Banc d'Arguin heritage site, where UNESCO is working with the Government of Mauritania to seek designation as a "Particularly Sensitive Sea Area" (PSSA) under the International Maritime Organisation (IMO) guidelines, and the adoption of associated protective measures to mitigate the potential impacts from shipping.

In addition to these core roles, UNESCO also plays a complementary role in supporting national governments and authorities in their conservation efforts. Through its World Heritage Marine Programme, established in 2005, UNESCO ensures that World Heritage Marine sites represent exemplary models of conservation and good governance, and provides capacity building and training, in addition to knowledge sharing and networking between site managers. What is UNESCO's role in marine conservation?

The primary objective of the 1972 World Heritage Convention is to protect cultural and natural heritage of "outstanding universal value" (OUV).

UNESCO's role in marine conservation is therefore to ensure that:

     1.    Sites in the marine environment that host cultural and natural heritage of OUV are identified and inscribed on the World Heritage List; and

     2.    The marine biodiversity of these sites is protected, along with their associated cultural, spiritual and economic values.

A recent example of UNESCO's role can be seen in the case of the Banc d'Arguin heritage site, where UNESCO is working with the Government of Mauritania to seek designation as a "Particularly Sensitive Sea Area" (PSSA) under the International Maritime Organisation (IMO) guidelines, and the adoption of associated protective measures to mitigate the potential impacts from shipping.

Similarly, the Programme has worked with the Government of Belize to support their efforts to remove the Belize Barrier Reef from the List of World Heritage in Danger, resulting in the prohibition of oil and gas exploration in the site.

In addition to these core roles, UNESCO also plays a complementary role in supporting national governments and authorities in their conservation efforts. Through its World Heritage Marine Programme, established in 2005, UNESCO ensures that World Heritage Marine sites represent exemplary models of conservation and good governance, and provides capacity building and training, in addition to knowledge sharing and networking between site managers. For example, the Programme has established a network of site managers that provides networking opportunities and a space to share best practices and experiences.

The Bahrain Action Plan for Marine World Heritage (2010) envisages that these roles will be strengthened and expanded, for example through improving data on marine protected areas and reviewing Tentative Lists to assist States in the inscription of new sites in gap provinces.

** Working across borders to steward our shared ocean heritage
------------------------------------------------------------

After a whirlwind month of travel, including stops in Mexico, Costa Rica, Chile and the United States, I am heartened to see nations around the world stepping up cooperating to enhance conservation of World Heritage marine sites. The Fourth International Marine Protected Areas Congress (IMPAC4) issued a call to action to strengthen protection of marine areas of Outstanding Universal Value, and we are seeing leaders across the globe rise to the occasion

- ███████, Coordinator, World Heritage Marine Programme

** Presidents of China and Mexico join forces to halt illegal totoaba trade and save the vaquita
------------------------------------------------------------

Last month, the World Heritage marine programme participated in the first trilateral meeting between Mexico, USA and China to halt the illegal Totoaba trade. All three nations agreed to step up joint action to combat trafficking of this Mexican fish, which threatens the critically endangered vaquita often killed as bycatch. A week later, when Mexican President Enrique Peña Nieto and Chinese President Xi Jinping met during the Summit of Emerging Economies and Developing Countries in Xiamen City, both leaders reiterated their commitment to the conservation of endangered species. This international cooperation embodies the spirit of World Heritage.

** IMPAC4 calls nations to strengthen conservation of World Heritage sites
------------------------------------------------------------

Earlier this month, we attended the Fourth International Marine Protected Areas Congress (IMPAC4) in Chile. In partnership with the French Agency for Biodiversity, the World Heritage marine programme presented its latest research on climate impacts on the world's most iconic coral reefs, as well as World Heritage potential in the High Seas. The official IMPAC4 Call to Action urges stronger protection of World Heritage marine sites, thereby emphasizing the importance of the World Heritage network for achieving the 2030 Sustainable Development Agenda.

** East Rennell: Ministers commit to end logging to get off Danger List
------------------------------------------------------------
Following the first ever round table with customary owners and local communities of East Rennell last month, ministers of the Solomon Islands confirmed their commitment to ban all logging in the World Heritage marine site. East Rennell was inscribed on the World Heritage List in Danger in 2013 because of logging, invasive rats and over-exploitation of its marine resources. Following our technical mission in 2015, the country submitted a desired state of conservation that set the conditions to get off the Danger List. This move is the first major step forward. Our work in East Rennell is supported by the government of Flanders, Belgium.

Read more (http://whc.unesco.org/en/news/1719)

** World Heritage managers in Latin America step up collaboration
------------------------------------------------------------
Last year, managers of seven Latin American sites committed to increased cooperation to improve conservation of the Eastern Tropical Pacific marine corridor, and combat illegal fishing across seven marine World Heritage sites. This August, managers met in Guanacaste, Costa Rica in an effort to step up regional conservation projects. Several concrete ideas emerged, including installation of more radars for remote surveillance, and a cooperative program to eradicate invasive species. The funding-platform PACIFICO will now work with the World Heritage sites to take the work forward.

Read more (http://whc.unesco.org/en/news/1718)

** Coming Next: World Heritage Coral Reefs exhibit in Paris
------------------------------------------------------------
UNESCO's World Heritage Centre and the Aquarium of Paris are launching a major new exhibit on "Coral Reefs on UNESCO's World Heritage List" starting on 3 October. Through photo panels, short films and virtual reality experiences, the exhibit will showcase the Outstanding Universal Value of World Heritage coral reefs and shed light on the devastating impacts of climate change on these iconic places. Special educational events will be held for families and children on the effects of climate change and coral reefs.
