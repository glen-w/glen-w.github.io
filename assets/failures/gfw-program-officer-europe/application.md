Briefing note
Fisheries transparency and the work of Global Fishing Watch
● Overfishing, pollution and climate change are impacting the Iberian coast, threatening ecosystems that support the fishing and tourism industries.
● Fisheries transparency benefits actors across the value chain by reducing monitoring costs, improving traceability, and supporting scientific research and innovation.
● Global Fishing Watch uses cutting-edge technology to track commercial fishing, increasing transparency and helping to improve fisheries management.

The benefits of fisheries transparency
Increasing transparency benefits actors across the fisheries value chain:
● Governments can more easily identify vessels engaged in illegal, unreported, and unregulated (IUU) fishing and other crimes at sea.
● Seafood suppliers and retailers can see where and how fish are caught, facilitating efforts to source from boats that operate legally and responsibly.
● Researchers can better study ocean health, identify vulnerable areas and evaluate the effectiveness of conservation measures and fisheries policies.
● NGOs and journalists can identify and investigate suspicious vessels, and advocate for stronger environmental protection.
● Fishers can demonstrate that they are operating legally and responsibly, enabling them to access markets for sustainable, traceable seafood.

### Introducing Global Fishing Watch

We are an independent non-profit organization dedicated to advancing ocean sustainability through increased fisheries transparency. GFW was founded in 2015 through a collaboration between Oceana, SkyTruth and Google. We use cutting-edge technology to visualise, track and share data about global fishing activity. The GFW platform supports scientific research and advocacy, helping to tackle overfishing and improve fisheries management.
How it works
① We collect public vessel tracking data and partner with governments to share proprietary monitoring data. ② Our experts “teach” algorithms to identify fishing activity and look for patterns. ③ Our public map allows anyone to monitor commercial fishing, for free, in near real-time.
Our impact
● Using GFW data and analysis during a patrol in the North Pacific, the US Coast Guard produced an eightfold increase in identified violations.
● Cooperating with Indonesian and Panamanian authorities, we supported INTERPOL’s efforts to apprehend a notorious vessel wanted in several jurisdictions.
● Collaborating with the FAO and other partners, we published groundbreaking analysis of commercial fishing and transshipment, illuminating a practice that can mask illegal activity.
● Incorporating GFW data into Bloomberg Terminal, a mainstream business platform that reaches hundreds of thousands of users, to support investment and innovation in sustainable fisheries.
